3.5 Neuron Models


Biological neurons are cells with a highly intricate structure and complex electrophysiological dynamics, and the brain contains many neuron types differing in morphology and behavior. In computational neuroscience, different types of neuron models serve specific modeling purposes.

### Two model families

| Family                | Output                                                     |
| --------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Analog neuron models  | Real-valued output, interpretable as the neuron's firing rate |
| Spiking neuron models | Impulses corresponding to the spikes emitted by biological neurons |

### Discovery of neurons

Early theories claimed that nerves distribute brain fluid throughout the body. The neuron doctrine, and with it modern neuroscience, was founded in the 19th century.

| Year | Contribution                                                                                          |
| ---- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1873 | Camillo Golgi develops the silver nitrate method, making the morphology of neural cells in tissue probes visible |
| 1889 | Santiago Ramón y Cajal improves Golgi's staining methods and documents the microscopic structure of the nervous system in a famous series of drawings |

### Levels of modeling

After Herz et al. (2006), single-neuron models span five levels of detail:

| Level | Model type                    | Description                                                                            |
| ----- | ----------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| I     | Detailed compartmental models | Capture detailed morphology from anatomical reconstructions; geometry discretized into compartments, each modeled as a point neuron |
| II    | Reduced compartmental models  | Only a few dendritic compartments, lower computational complexity, higher analytical tractability |
| III   | Single-compartment models     | Ignore morphology; detailed spiking point neurons capture ion currents and spike generation |
| IV    | Cascade models                | Purely functional, represent abstract computations without biological detail            |
| V     | Black-box models              | No computational function; neuron as input/output system with defined statistic behavior |

### Remarks

- Both the McCulloch-Pitts cell and the LIF neuron are point neuron models that do not capture cell morphology.
- More complex analog neuron models are based on differential equations rather than activation functions.
