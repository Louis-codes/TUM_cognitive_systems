3.6 Whole-Brain Modeling


Whole brain models are executable hypotheses of the architecture and the functioning of the brain. They integrate a large number of simulated neuron models in a coherent network model that captures the connectivity of biological brains at varying levels of detail.

### Motivation and challenges

- The brain is organized on multiple levels, from proteins and chromosomes (nanometers) over synapses, cells, microcircuits and brain regions to the whole brain and the body (meters).
- Central questions of brain research: What does it mean to understand the brain? Which aspects of brain organization are relevant, which can be neglected? Are current neuroscientific tools sufficient? Which and how much data is required?
- Analogy (Jonas & Kording 2017): applying standard neuroscience methods (connectivity analysis, lesion studies, activity statistics) to a fully understood MOS 6502 microprocessor does "not lead to a meaningful understanding of the processor" in terms of its information processing hierarchy, although a hypothesis "would be reasonably easy to test".

### Types of whole brain models

| Type                             | Construction                                                          |
| -------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Simplified engineered models     | Defined manually based on rules and statistics identified in datasets   |
| Detailed data-driven models      | Reconstructed from data based on an automatic toolchain                 |

Many whole brain models are large-scale simulations of building blocks of the thalamocortical system. Detailed modeling at large scale requires considerable processing power and storage and became feasible only in recent years.

### Prominent examples

| Year | Model                                | Key facts                                                                                                   |
| ---- | ------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1992 | Visual cortex (Tononi et al.)        | Task-tailored engineered simulation with nine functionally segregated areas; 10,000 neuronal groups, 1,000,000 connections; color camera input; solves the binding problem |
| 2008 | Thalamocortical system (Izhikevich et al.) | Engineered model based on DTI and 3D neuron reconstructions; 1 million spiking multi-compartment neurons, ≈ 500 million synapses, 22 neuron types; reproduces typical patterns of brain activity |
| 2012 | Spaun (Eliasmith et al.)             | Functional spiking model synthesized from a cognitive architecture (Neural Engineering Framework, Semantic Pointer Architecture); 2.5 million spiking neurons; focus on production of behavior |
| 2015 | Blue Brain Project (Markram et al.)  | Data-driven reconstruction process for cortical microcircuits; 31,000 anatomically detailed neurons, 37 million synapses; digital reconstruction from data and predictions, reproduces experimental recordings |

### Intermediate summary

All these models were built on a specific set of assumptions and for a specific purpose. They are largely decoupled and correspond to different research directions even though they represent the same ground truth, and manual engineering makes it hard to incorporate new findings. This motivates the need for a flexible unified model that captures all available knowledge about the brain and can be extended with new data.
