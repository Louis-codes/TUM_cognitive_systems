3.1.1 Cerebrum & Cerebral Cortex


The cerebrum (Großhirn) is the largest and uppermost portion of the brain. It accounts for sensory integration, voluntary motion and higher-level cognitive functions.

### Basic structure

| Element                          | German             | Description                                                            |
| -------------------------------- | ------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Two hemispheres                  |                    | Connected by the corpus callosum (Balken)                              |
| Cerebral cortex / gray matter    | Großhirnrinde / graue Substanz | Folded outer layer, mainly comprised of cell bodies         |
| White matter                     | weiße Substanz     | Inner core of nerve fibers that connects the cortical regions          |

### Subdivision of the cortical surface

The surface of the cerebral cortex is subdivided based on three anatomical features:

| Feature            | German     | Definition                                                        |
| ------------------ | ---------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Gyri (sg. Gyrus)   | Windungen  | Crests formed by the convoluted surface of the cortex             |
| Sulci (sg. Sulcus) | Furchen    | Fissures between two neighboring gyri                             |
| Lobes              | Lappen     | Two major sulci divide each hemisphere into frontal, parietal, temporal and occipital lobe |

Landmarks: central sulcus (Zentralfurche) between precentral gyrus (primary motor cortex) and postcentral gyrus (primary sensory cortex); lateral sulcus separating the temporal lobe.
