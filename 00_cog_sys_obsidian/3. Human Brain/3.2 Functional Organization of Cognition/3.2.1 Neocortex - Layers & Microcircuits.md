3.2.1 Neocortex - Layers & Microcircuits


The neocortex is part of the cerebral cortex and considered to be the center of higher cognitive functions and intelligence. Most parts are organized in six layers (from outside to inside).

### The six cortical layers

| Layer | Name                          | German                      | Content and role                                                                 |
| ----- | ----------------------------- | --------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| I     | Molecular Layer               | Molekularschicht            | Dendrites of cells in deeper layers and axons travelling through from other cortical areas |
| II    | External Granular Cell Layer  | äußere Körnerzellenschicht  | Small pyramidal-shaped cells; projections within the same and to other cortical areas |
| III   | External Pyramidal Cell Layer | äußere Pyramidenschicht     | Similar to layer II; neurons deeper in the layer are typically larger             |
| IV    | Internal Granule Cell Layer   | innere Körnerzellenschicht  | Many small spherical neurons; main recipient of sensory input from the thalamus   |
| V     | Internal Pyramidal Cell Layer | innere Pyramidenschicht     | Large pyramidal cells projecting to other cortical and subcortical areas; major output pathways of the cortex |
| VI    | Multiform Layer               | multiforme Schicht          | Heterogeneous neurons, blends into the white matter                              |

### Cortical microcircuit

In addition to the laminar structure, the cortex is organized in columns (Kolumne/Säule).

- The most basic building block is the minicolumn, consisting of 80–100 neurons.
- Cortical columns or modules are comprised of many minicolumns.
