1.2 Prerequisites for Modeling Cognition


Cognitive science is inherently interdisciplinary, and any model of cognition must make explicit how much biology it borrows, which kind of explanation it gives, and at which level of abstraction it operates.

### Relevant fields of research

| Engineering | Natural Sciences | Humanities |
| ----------- | ---------------- | ---------- |
| Artificial Intelligence, Machine Learning, Computer Science, Robotics & Control, Chip Design, subtopics of Mathematics | Neuroscience, Neurobiology, Developmental Sciences, Biomechanics, Neural Engineering, Neuroimaging | Cognitive Psychology, Linguistics, Epistemology, Philosophy, Ethics |

### Biological inspiration

Cognitive systems and functions can be modeled at different degrees of biological realism:

| Model class | Description |
| ----------- | ----------- |
| Computational models | Implement cognitive functions solely from a functional view of the system, without reference to biology |
| Bio-inspired models | Implement cognitive functions by replicating known or hypothesized mechanisms of cognitive processing in biological organisms |
| Hybrid models | Combine computational and bio-inspired modeling |

Vernon (2014) spans these along two axes, level of abstraction and biological inspiration: a modular decomposition of a hypothetical model of mind sits high and low, statistical learning of domain rules low and low, artificial neural networks low and high, and a system modeled on the macroscopic organization of the brain high and high.

### The ultimate-proximate distinction

Cognition is specific to an agent and its environment. In evolution, living creatures developed cognitive skills that let them act and survive in their native environments, so every biological cognitive function has two explanations:

| Explanation | Question |
| ----------- | -------- |
| Ultimate | Why does a cognitive system exhibit a certain behavior? |
| Proximate | How is that behavior implemented in the cognitive system? |

**Definition 3.1**: a specific behavior of a biological cognitive system has both an ultimate and a proximate explanation. Ultimate explanations concern why a behavior exists, proximate explanations how it works. Both are complementary and the distinction is critical to evolutionary explanation (adapted from Scott-Phillips et al., 2011).

Example (crying baby): ultimately, crying elicits care and defense from caregivers, and babies that do not cry when in need have a lower chance of surviving. Proximately, babies cry because of separation from caregivers, cold, lack of food and other internal mechanisms.

Consequence: developing a cognitive system successfully requires that the implementation of cognitive functions with ultimate explanations matches the specific working environment of the system.

### Levels of abstraction

Every cognitive system can be modeled at three hierarchical levels, loosely or tightly coupled depending on the modeling paradigm (Vernon, 2014):

| Level | Content |
| ----- | ------- |
| Computational theory | High-level description stating the goal of the computation and the logic of the strategy by which it is carried out |
| Representation and algorithms | Realization of the computational theory in terms of input, output, representations and the algorithms performing the transformations |
| Hardware implementation | Physical implementation of the representations and algorithms |
