1.1.1 Neuroimaging Methods


Neuroimaging provides the tools to observe cognition in the living brain. Structural methods image the anatomy of tissue, functional methods monitor brain activity.

### Structural neuroimaging

| Method | Principle |
| ------ | --------- |
| Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) | Non-invasive imaging of the anatomic structure of tissue in vivo; active sensing based on the magnetic properties of hydrogen atoms, which dissipate energy differently depending on the surrounding tissue |
| Diffusion Tensor Imaging (DTI) | Extension of MRI that measures the diffusion movement of water molecules and thereby detects nerve fibers |

Both methods are based on nuclear magnetic resonance.

### Functional neuroimaging

| Method | Principle | Strength |
| ------ | --------- | -------- |
| Electroencephalography (EEG) | Measures electrical potentials from neural activity via electrodes on the skull | High temporal resolution, non-invasive |
| Magnetoencephalography (MEG) | Detects the magnetic field corresponding to neural activity | High temporal resolution, non-invasive |
| PET and SPECT | Nuclear imaging techniques requiring medication with radiotracers; activity monitored implicitly via regional cerebral blood flow | Metabolic view |
| Functional MRI (fMRI) | Extension of MRI visualizing changes of the blood oxygen level caused by activity elicited through external stimuli or cognitive processing | High spatial resolution |

### Haptic functional brain imaging

Haptic fMRI augments fMRI with a haptic input device: the test person in the scanner looks at virtual objects projected on a screen, the haptic device enables interaction with those objects, and the resulting brain activity (for example in the motor cortex) is monitored via fMRI.
