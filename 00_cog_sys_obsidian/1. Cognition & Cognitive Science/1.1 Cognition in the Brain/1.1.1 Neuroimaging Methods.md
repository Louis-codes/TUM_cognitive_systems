## Structural

**MRI (Magnetic Resonance Imaging)**  
Non-invasive imaging of the anatomical structure of tissue in vivo, based on the magnetic properties of hydrogen atoms, which dissipate energy differently depending on the surrounding tissue.

**DTI (Diffusion Tensor Imaging)**  
Extension of MRI

## Functional

**EEG (Electroencephalography)**  
Measures electrical potentials generated by neural activity via electrodes placed on the skull. Non-invasive with very high temporal resolution.

**PET (Positron Emission Tomography)**  
Nuclear imaging technique requiring medication with radiotracers; activity is monitored implicitly via regional cerebral blood flow. Provides a metabolic view of brain function.

**fMRI (Functional Magnetic Resonance Imaging)**  
Extension of MRI visualizing changes in blood oxygen level caused by activity elicited through external stimuli or cognitive processing. High spatial resolution.
