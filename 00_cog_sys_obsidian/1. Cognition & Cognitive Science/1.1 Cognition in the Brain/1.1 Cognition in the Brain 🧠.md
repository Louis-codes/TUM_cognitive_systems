1.1 Cognition in the Brain


A central question of cognitive science is how cognition is reflected in the structure and function of the brain. Historically, the answer developed from speculative faculty psychology over cytoarchitectonic maps to modern digital brain atlases.

### Phrenology

Phrenology, from grk. *phren* (mind) and *logos* (knowledge), was a predecessor of cognitive science founded by the German physician Franz Joseph Gall at the end of the 18th century. It described the mind based on assumptions about the anatomical structure of the brain:


### Motor and sensory regions of the cerebral cortex

Primary motor cortex (precentral gyrus) and primary sensory cortex (postcentral gyrus), the somatic motor association area (premotor cortex) and the somatic sensory association area, prefrontal cortex, auditory cortex and auditory association area, visual cortex and visual association area, plus the two speech regions: Broca's area (production of speech) and Wernicke's area (understanding of speech).
