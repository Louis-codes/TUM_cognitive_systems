5.4 The Perceptron


The perceptron is a linear classifier based on a single artificial neuron with a digital threshold function. It is the first example of a supervised learning rule and separates two classes of points through a line.

### The artificial neuron

An analog artificial neuron is defined by its synaptic weights and an activation function. With activation function $A$, weights $w$, bias $b$ and input $x$:

$$n(x) = A(w_1x_1 + \dots + w_nx_n + b) = A(w^Tx + b)$$

### Perceptron and perceptron criterion

$$y(x) = f(w^Tx) \quad \text{with} \quad f(x) = \begin{cases} +1 & x \ge 0 \\ -1 & x < 0 \end{cases}$$

With $y_n \in \{-1,1\}$, all correctly classified samples fulfil $(w^Tx_n)y_n > 0$. The perceptron criterion punishes only the incorrectly classified samples $n \in \mathcal{M}$:

$$E_P(w) = -\sum_{n \in \mathcal{M}} (w^Tx_n)y_n$$

### The learning rule

The gradient is $\nabla E_P(w) = -\sum_{n \in \mathcal{M}} x_n y_n$. Applying stochastic gradient descent with learning rate $\eta$ gives the online weight update $\Delta w = \eta x_n y_n$ and therefore

$$w^{t+1} = w^t + \eta x_n y_n \ \text{for all } n \in \mathcal{M} \qquad w^{t+1} = w^t + \eta'\big(y_n - f^t(x_n)\big)x_n \ \text{for all } n$$

### Convergence

A single update step reduces the error of the corresponding misclassified sample. In general:

- if the dataset is linearly separable, the algorithm finds a solution within a finite number of steps (perceptron convergence theorem),
- the computed solution depends on the initialization of the parameters and the order of presentation of the training samples,
- the algorithm does not converge for datasets that are not linearly separable.

### The XOR problem

The perceptron cannot classify the XOR dataset, since it is not linearly separable. This finding led to an AI winter and the widespread abandonment of connectionism for almost two decades.
